My Goal

To strengthen the office of Area D Director and leave Roberts Creek better for having had the election.

This campaign is about giving Roberts Creek a real choice and demonstrating a deliberate, accountable approach to local government. Whatever the outcome on October 17, I want this election to strengthen the expectations we place on the office and the connection between the Director and the community.

Priorities

Practical goals for Roberts Creek to avoid the reacting trap and stay on the governing path.

Protect Our Watershed & Aquifer 555

Roberts Creek has a long history of advocating for the protection of our watershed and groundwater. The next step should be an explicit watershed-protection objective. We should work with First Nations, the Province, water users, the scientific community and conservation groups to establish what we are protecting, build the monitoring necessary to understand its condition, and then pursue the appropriate tools under the Water Sustainability Act. That should include examining legally enforceable Water Objectives and, where the evidence and risks warrant it, a Water Sustainability Plan.

Renew the OCP Without Losing Roberts Creek

A more integrated OCP can make planning simpler, more consistent and more efficient, and that is an opportunity worth taking. But Area D’s existing OCP should remain the foundation for the Roberts Creek portion of the new plan, updated through meaningful community participation to reflect current conditions and provincial requirements. Local involvement through bodies such as the RCOCPC should be preserved, and greater regional efficiency should not come at the expense of legitimate local policy choices or accountability.

Turn the Water Strategy Into Water in the Pipes

The SCRD has now adopted a credible long-term water strategy built around diversified supply, resilient infrastructure, efficiency and source protection. The priority should be disciplined execution: clear project milestones, urgency, transparent costs and regular public reporting on what each investment delivers. Progress is being made, but the job is not finished until dependable water is available through increasingly severe dry summers.

Plan the Future of Fire Protection

Establish a clear, updated long-term plan for the fire service Roberts Creek needs and can sustain. Define the service model, staffing, equipment, facilities and replacement needs, forecast their costs, and explain clearly to residents why those investments are required. Major budget changes should emerge from a plan, not arrive as surprises.

Make Decisions on Waste & Recycling

The SCRD now has a credible long-term direction for reducing waste and extending the life of the existing landfill. The priority should be disciplined implementation, strong environmental oversight for the community that hosts the landfill, and bringing unresolved service questions to a conclusion. Curbside recycling should finally receive a clear evidence-based decision using current costs, diversion benefits, accessibility and service equity, not another cycle of study and deferral.

Steward Our Parks & Community Lands

Protect Roberts Creek’s parks and community lands through deliberate long-term stewardship. Use the current parks review to clarify how these assets should be protected, maintained and used. Understand their legal protections and future needs, engage the community before significant changes are committed, and ensure major proposals are considered within clear park plans and an agreed community vision rather than being allowed to define that vision themselves.

Make Development Approvals Predictable

The SCRD has begun important work to modernize its development approval system. Finish it and measure the results. Establish clear service standards, track where applications spend their time, publish performance against those standards, and ensure technical, environmental and regulatory requirements are applied consistently and followed through. Good oversight should be thorough without being unnecessarily slow. A clear, predictable and accountable approval system is essential to responsible development and the housing our community needs.

Make Room for the Housing Roberts Creek Needs

Roberts Creek needs more attainable choices for renters, families, seniors and people trying to establish a home in the community. Use the OCP renewal to identify where additional housing can be supported by water, wastewater, transportation and environmental capacity, and make appropriate housing easier to build. Measure success by homes actually created, not theoretical zoning capacity, while ensuring growth strengthens rather than erodes the character of Roberts Creek.

Improve Transit & Safe Connections

The SCRD should focus on the transportation tools it can actually influence: reliable transit, handyDART, better stops and connections, and land-use planning that makes those services more useful. The 2027 Transit Future Action Plan is an opportunity to define the next improvements and measure whether they are working. Where roads and active transportation fall under provincial jurisdiction, the SCRD should establish clear local priorities and advocate consistently for them.

Strengthen Emergency Resilience

The SCRD has made important progress identifying the hazards facing our region and improving emergency preparedness. The next step is to turn that knowledge into action. Complete regional evacuation planning, strengthen neighbourhood and response capacity, identify and protect critical infrastructure, and ensure known wildfire, flood and other hazards inform future land-use decisions. Good emergency management means reducing foreseeable risks before an emergency forces us to react.

Govern Recreation for the Long Term

Recreation and cultural services are the largest single share of Area D's SCRD tax dollars, accounting for about 35% in 2026. Spending at that scale deserves clear annual public reporting that brings together facility use, operating costs, condition, capital needs and long-term viability. The Board should review major facilities against clear service goals, identify risks before they become urgent, and adjust plans as conditions change. Recreation should be governed as a major public service, not allowed to drift until a facility or funding problem becomes the next crisis.

Who is Scott?

Scott Tilley, AScT , lives and works in Roberts Creek, where he has built both his professional life and his family life.

His work ranges from hands-on marine service on the Sunshine Coast to scientific and technical work tracking satellites and studying radio signals from space for an international clientele. In 2018, working from his home station, Scott rediscovered NASA's long-lost IMAGE spacecraft. His work has since been featured by NASA and covered by The Washington Post , BBC , CBC , Vancouver Sun , Coast Reporter , NPR , India Today , Sixth Tone and media around the world.

The subjects vary, but Scott's working method is consistent: observe carefully, test assumptions, document the evidence, measure what is happening, correct course when the evidence demands it, and follow the problem through to a result. That is the same discipline he believes local government should apply.

Scott has also served in professional and volunteer governance roles. He currently serves as Vice President of AMSAT-CA, Canada’s amateur satellite organization, helping with governance, partnerships and technical projects. He previously served for several years on the Practice Review Board of ASTTBC, the provincial professional association for applied science technologists and technicians, adjudicating professional standards and practice issues.

Scott grew up with an early view of public service through his father's work as an elected public utility commissioner. That experience, together with his own work in science, consulting and community issues, shaped a simple view of government: define the goal, make the plan, assign responsibility, execute, measure, finish, and keep testing whether the process is working.

He is running for Area D Director with one goal: to strengthen the office and leave Roberts Creek better for having had the election.
